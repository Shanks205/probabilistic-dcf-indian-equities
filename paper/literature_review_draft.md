## 1. Discounted Cash Flow Valuation

Discounted cash flow valuation is a fundamental intrinsic valuation method that estimates the present value of future cash flows. In corporate valuation, the free cash flow to firm approach discounts operating cash flows available to all providers of capital using the weighted average cost of capital. The method is theoretically grounded but highly sensitive to forecast assumptions such as revenue growth, margins, reinvestment needs, discount rates, and terminal value.

## 2. Terminal Value Sensitivity

In many DCF models, a large portion of enterprise value comes from the terminal value. This makes the model highly sensitive to the assumed terminal growth rate and WACC. Small changes in terminal assumptions can create large changes in estimated fair value. This is especially important for high-growth and high-multiple companies where the market price may already imply a long competitive-advantage period.

## 3. Valuation Under Uncertainty

Traditional deterministic DCF models produce one estimated value, but business outcomes rarely follow one path. Revenue growth, operating margins, reinvestment, working capital, and discount rates are uncertain. A probabilistic valuation framework recognizes this uncertainty by modeling the valuation output as a distribution rather than a point estimate.

## 4. Monte Carlo Simulation in Valuation

Monte Carlo simulation allows valuation inputs to vary across assumed ranges or distributions. Instead of producing one fair value, the model generates many possible fair values. This helps estimate downside scenarios, upside scenarios, median fair value, and the probability that intrinsic value exceeds the observed market price.

## 5. Emerging Market Valuation

Emerging-market valuation can be more uncertain because of higher macro volatility, changing cost of capital, liquidity differences, regulatory risk, commodity exposure, and corporate-governance variation. Indian equities also include a wide range of business models, from mature cash-flow businesses to high-growth compounders and commodity-linked cyclicals. This makes a probabilistic valuation framework useful for comparing uncertainty across sectors.

## 6. Business Quality Versus Valuation Risk

A central theme in this project is the distinction between business quality and valuation-expectation risk. High-quality businesses may have strong brands, high ROCE, stable margins, strong order books, or long growth runways. However, if the market price already discounts optimistic assumptions, the investment may still show limited margin of safety under conservative DCF assumptions.

## 7. Research Gap

Many public valuation projects focus on deterministic DCF output. Fewer projects combine:

1. Indian listed-company sample selection,
2. FCFF-based deterministic valuation,
3. Monte Carlo intrinsic-value distributions,
4. risk-matrix interpretation,
5. cross-company valuation-expectation comparison,
6. reproducible GitHub structure,
7. SSRN-style research documentation.

This project attempts to fill that practical research gap.
