# Sample Selection Protocol

## Purpose

This document explains how the 12-company sample is selected for the probabilistic DCF valuation project. The sample is designed for research and valuation-methodology testing, not for direct investment recommendation.

## Sample Design

The sample contains two groups:

1. **Retained companies** from the earlier Indian equity valuation framework.
2. **Fresh-screened companies** selected using Screener-style fundamental filters.

This hybrid method provides continuity with earlier research while adding new companies for originality and broader valuation testing.

## Universe

The intended universe is Indian listed non-financial companies with sufficient public disclosures and enough liquidity to support public-source valuation work.

## Exclusions

The following are excluded from this FCFF DCF framework:

- banks,
- NBFCs,
- insurance companies,
- highly regulated utilities,
- companies with very low liquidity,
- companies with insufficient public disclosures,
- companies whose reported earnings are dominated by non-recurring one-off items.

Financial companies are excluded because they require different valuation approaches.

## Fresh Screener-Style Selection Criteria

Indicative screening filters:

```text
Market capitalization > Rs 1,000 crore
Debt to equity < 0.75
ROCE > 15%
ROE > 10%
Interest coverage > 3
Operating profit margin > 8%
Positive operating cash flow
Reasonable free cash flow record
Promoter holding > 30%
Current ratio > 1
Sufficient public disclosures
```

## Scoring Framework

Each candidate can be scored using the following framework:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Profitability quality | 20 |
| Cash-flow conversion | 20 |
| Balance-sheet strength | 15 |
| Growth consistency | 15 |
| Valuation reasonableness | 15 |
| Business-model clarity | 10 |
| Data availability | 5 |

## Research Boundary

The sample is not a portfolio recommendation. It is a valuation-research sample designed to test how probabilistic DCF behaves across different company types.
